About TCsMusic

TCsMusic is a piano assistant for self-taught players who want to know why a chord works, not just where to put their fingers. It treats music like a language: notes are letters, chords are words, and progressions are sentences.

The site has five pages. Learn and Practice each hold several sections, so the menu stays short.

  • Assistant has a chord wheel that shows which chords go together, 25 chord types with a chart, scales, progressions and a live detector that names the chord you hold. The wheel also shows the vii° chord of every key, outlines the seven chords of the key you picked, and suggests which extensions fit each chord.
  • Learn has three sections. The method teaches letters, words and sentences. Scales covers major and minor, pentatonic and blues, the church modes, symmetrical scales and tonics, with the scale shown on the keyboard and on a staff, the chords each one makes, and a button that opens it in the Studio. Notation explains note values, dynamics, tempo and key signatures.
  • Practice has two sections. Ear & timing is an ear trainer and a slow-down-to-speed-up metronome. Songs plays pieces on the keys, loads your MIDI files and records you.
  • Studio is a small workstation: multi-track recording (instruments, drums, bass, guitar, sampler, your voice), a piano roll with a sustain pedal, a mixer with effects, sound effects, and WAV and stem export.

The key menu on Learn and Practice lists the black keys twice, once as a sharp and once as a flat (C♯ and D♭, for example), and notes are written with the spelling you pick. Every page shares the same keyboard, which slides up from the bottom of the screen to show what is playing when you scroll away from it. Old links to the Notation, Scales and Songs pages still work.

Studio & MIDI controller

The Studio starts with a transport bar (play, stop, record, tempo, loop length, undo and redo, metronome, export) and a list of tracks. Add as many as you like: Instrument, Drum Machine, Bass, Guitar, Sampler (load or record a sound and play it on the keys) and Voice / Audio (record from your microphone or import a file). Import audio turns every WAV, MP3 or OGG you pick into its own track and stretches the loop to fit it. The guitars are synthesized, not recorded samples.

The progress bar sits right above the editing views. It shows the time and the bar and beat, and you can click or drag it to jump. Below it are three views. The Sequencer is an 8-row drum grid (kick, snare, clap, hi-hat, open hat, tom, rim, cowbell). The Piano roll is where you write melodies, for loops of 1 to 16 bars, with a sustain pedal lane, a per-track Release, multi-select, copy and paste, and tools to transpose, quantize, humanize and make notes legato. The Mixer gives every track a volume fader, pan, mute, solo, reverb, echo, drive and tone, and a level meter.

Undo and redo cover every edit. Export WAV saves the whole mix and Export stems saves one file per track in a zip. Your work is saved in this browser.

Click a note on the roll to edit it: its sound, volume, a Slide that bends the pitch across the note, and fade in and out. Besides the piano sounds there are a Church Organ, Choir, Strings, Bells, Harp, Bass, Nylon and Electric Guitar, chip sounds and a Dark Drone, plus a Pure Sine, a Noise Sweep and a Gong for building sound effects. Tick Sound-effect mode and the drums play once, and Export WAV saves a single take with the silence trimmed and the loudness matched. Pick a key and scale in the roll's toolbar to shade it on the roll (the Scales section in Learn can send one straight here). The tempo goes up to 300 BPM.

Using a MIDI controller (made for the M-Vave SMK-37 Pro)

Connect MIDI
Use Chrome, Edge or Opera and allow MIDI access when asked
Keys
Play the keyboard. It follows your notes, and the pitch wheel, mod wheel and sustain pedal work straight away
Pads
Pads on MIDI channel 10 play the 16 on-screen pads. With Record on, your hits go into the sequencer
Learn
Press Learn, click any control on the page, then move a knob, fader, pad or button on your keyboard to link them
Record
Record keys into the piano roll and pads into the sequencer while the loop plays

You can link any knob, fader or button to play and stop, record, tempo, loop length, octave, master volume, and each track's volume, pan, mute and solo. Your links are remembered. The site remembers that your keyboard was connected and reconnects by itself on the next page, as long as your browser has already allowed MIDI. Because each page loads fresh, the connection restarts when you change pages rather than staying open.

Reference

Keyboard and controls

Space
Full sustain pedal while held. On the Studio page it plays and pauses instead
Shift
Half pedal while held
Click a key
Play it, click a lit key again to unlight it
Drag across keys
Glissando with the mouse or your finger
Octave arrows
Slide the keyboard to another octave. Each C is labelled (C3, C4, C5…)
Typing keys
Play the keyboard from your computer keys. Press them to hear notes, switch them off with the Typing keys button
Chord wheel
Click a chord to see its neighbours. Switch between chord relationships and scale degrees
Sound
Switch between piano, e-piano, organ, synth, harpsichord, music box, church organ, choir, strings, bells, harp, chip sounds and more
Clear keys
Remove every highlight at once
Sun / moon icon
Switch between dark and light mode

The keyboard shows three octaves at a time and slides along all 88 keys of a piano, A0 to C8. Play a note beyond the edge and it slides there for you.

Words worth knowing

Semitone
The distance between two neighbouring keys, black or white.
Root
The note a chord or scale is named after.
Triad
A three-note chord: root, third and fifth.
Progression
A sequence of chords, the “sentence” of a song.
Sustain
Letting notes keep ringing after you lift your fingers.
