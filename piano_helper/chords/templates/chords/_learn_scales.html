Tonics: the home note

The tonic is the note a scale is named after, the one that feels like home. The tonic chord is the chord built on it. Tunes usually start there, and end there too.

Chords in a key do three jobs. Tonic (T) chords feel settled. Subdominant (S) chords lift away from home. Dominant (D) chords create tension that wants to fall back home.

Relative keys share the same notes but start in a different place, like C major and A minor. Parallel keys share the same tonic but use different notes, like C major and C minor.

Symmetrical scales (whole-tone, diminished, augmented, chromatic) repeat their own pattern, so no note stands out as the home. Any of their notes can act as the root, and that is why they sound like they are floating.
